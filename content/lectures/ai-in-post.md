Two uses of AI are already part of audio postproduction: dialogue isolation and
sound-effect generation.

Dialogue-isolation tools separate speech from complex background noise that
ReaFIR may not handle well. Commercial options include [iZotope RX Dialogue
Isolate](https://www.izotope.com/en/products/rx/features) and [Waves Clarity
Vx](https://www.waves.com/plugins/clarity-vx). Browser-based services with free
tiers include [Adobe Enhance Speech](https://podcast.adobe.com/en/enhancespeech)
and [ElevenLabs Voice Isolator](https://elevenlabs.io/voice-isolator). These tools
add another step to the repair process from the [dialogue editing
lesson](/lectures/dialogue-editing/). They may recover lines that would otherwise
need ADR.

Text-to-SFX tools generate sound effects from written prompts.

## Questions for discussion

- If isolation keeps improving, what happens to ADR sessions? To the alternate
  take habit?
- Foley depends on performance choices such as timing, weight, and character.
  Which of those choices can a generator reproduce, and where does it fall short?
- Who recorded the sounds these generators learned from, and were they asked?
- The [syllabus](/syllabus/) draws a line between processing your own
  recordings and generating new material. Where do dialogue isolation and
  text-to-SFX fall relative to that line?
