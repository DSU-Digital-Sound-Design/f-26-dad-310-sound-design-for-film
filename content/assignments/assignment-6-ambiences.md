## Your task

Build **one 30-second stereo ambience to a video clip**. Establish
a continuous environment with an ambience bed and selected details, including
**at least one meaningful offscreen source**. Make the sounds feel as though
they belong to the same place and support the scene's mood and perspective.

Work to picture, the way we did with the *Night of the Living Dead* cemetery
in the [demonstration](/lectures/ambience-workshop/). Watching the shot tells
you where the listener is, what lies beyond the frame, and where a detail can
land.

## Find a clip

Choose about 30 seconds that stays in one place. A cut within that place is
fine. Look for a setting where the environment matters and the scene isn't
dominated by dialogue.

- **Your final-project footage** is the best choice, so the ambience carries
  straight into your [final project](/assignments/final-project/).
- **Otherwise, use a public domain film from [archive.org](https://archive.org/).**
  The [Feature Films](https://archive.org/details/feature_films) and
  [Prelinger Archives](https://archive.org/details/prelinger) collections are
  good places to start. Check the item's license on its page, then download the
  MP4. Pick a different scene from the cemetery clip we used in class.

Import the video into REAPER on a track named `VIDEO`, mute or delete its
original audio, and trim it to your 30 seconds. Keep the layers editable so you
can revise and extend the ambience under the full scene later.

**Due Monday, November 30, via D2L by class.** Our
[instructor demonstration](/lectures/ambience-workshop/) is November 16;
watch and discuss the choices, then begin your own session outside class.
We confirm final-project footage November 23. No new recording trip over Thanksgiving recess is required.

## Build the environment

1. **Choose the place and listening perspective.** Decide the setting, time,
   and mood, and where the listener is. For example: inside an empty diner at
   night, hearing traffic through the windows while a refrigerator hums nearby.
   Use this to decide what should feel nearby or distant.
2. **Plan the layers.** Pick a continuous bed, a few selected details, and at
   least one source outside the frame that tells us about the larger
   environment, such as distant traffic, activity in an adjoining room, or an
   animal beyond the frame. Include only elements that serve the place; layer
   count itself does not earn credit.
3. **Choose your sources.** You may reuse your own recordings and edited sounds
   from Assignments 3 and 4, make new recordings where needed, or use credited
   library material. Keep a source list with each sound's creator or library,
   plus a link and license information for downloaded sounds. Synthesis is
   optional. A finished library ambience with minimal changes is not sufficient;
   demonstrate your own selection, editing, layering, and balancing.
4. **Edit and layer in REAPER.** Use the
   [LISTEN editing mantra](/lectures/week-7/listen-mantra/) to prepare your
   sources. Build a bed that stays continuous, then place details selectively.
   Use level, panning, EQ, and reverb where helpful to establish perspective.
   Make joins unobtrusive. Seamless looping is optional, and short fades at the
   file's beginning and end are fine. Check the export for unwanted gaps, clicks,
   or cut-off sounds.

This is an environment-building exercise. You do not need to finish the scene's
Foley, dialogue, music, or foreground action effects for this submission.

## Submit

Zip one folder containing:
